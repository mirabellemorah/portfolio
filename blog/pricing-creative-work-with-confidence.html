Pricing creative work with confidence

Three questions that help you price a project without guessing, and without undercharging.

Mirabelle Morah 2 min read · 5 November 2026

Share

Pricing is the part of creative work nobody teaches us. We learn the craft, then we are handed a client email and expected to name a number.

Here is a simple way to think about it. Imagine you are selling lemonade. You would not only count the lemons. You would count the sugar, the cups, the stand, your morning, and the fact that people queue for yours because it is better than everyone else's.

Question one: what is the result worth?

A short animation that helps a charity raise more money is worth more than the hours it took. Ask what the work will change for the client. If the answer is "a lot", your price can reflect that.

Question two: how long will it really take?

Take your first guess and add a third. Feedback rounds, file exports and waiting for replies all take time, and they are rarely free.

Question three: what would I be happy to say yes to?

If a price makes you groan when you say it out loud, it is too low. If it makes you nervous but excited, you are probably close.

A price is not an apology. It is a clear statement of what the work is worth.

A tiny example

Say a project takes 20 hours. You usually charge for those hours, but the result will be used for a whole year of campaigns. Charging only for the 20 hours ignores the year. Many people add a "usage" line so the price matches how widely the work will be used.

What to do next

Write your three answers down before the next client call. Say the number once, calmly, and then wait. The pause feels long, but it is where good deals are made.
