## ***What’s inside?***

*This right here is about strategizing, managing and leading global campaigns with a case study on the first-ever People and Planet First Week campaign which I led. This is written in both documentary and educational formats. Not only am I documenting my process leading this global campaign, but I am also explaining to you why certain steps were taken and were important.*

- *About the campaign*
- *First steps taken and tools used*
- *Frustrations and measuring what is learned*
- *Replicating what is loved and taking stock*

**Role**: Communications Manager

**For who:** [People and Planet First](https://peopleandplanetfirst.org/) (stewarded by the [Social Enterprise World Forum](https://sewfonline.com/))

**Scope:** Global

**Tasks**: All round communications: social media content planning, campaign strategy, video editing, motion design, research, flyer/banner creation and printing, designing and sending newsletters, managing and designing web pages, editing blogs, virtual event moderation and organising virtual community meetups, setting up ad campaigns measuring analytics, managing relationships, writing reports, external speaking engagements, designing slide decks, etc.

By 18 November 2024, I was *2 months and 9 days (40 working days to be exact)* into my role as People and Planet First’s Communications manager. One of my major tasks was to ensure a successful run of this first-ever [People and Planet First Week](https://peopleandplanetfirst.org/highlights-from-people-and-planet-first-week-2024/) (18–22 November 2024). The week celebrated verified enterprises that put people and the planet first.

**First steps in working on the campaign**

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*dynnNv1hkGKFhF5S)

1.  **Get organised with a project management system:** the first thing I did was to set up my Notion account and add my workflow and tasks to this. Notion is a project management tool that allows you to sort your tasks, link to your calendar, assign roles to people, make comments, create charts, websites and even more. This thing is just legendary!
2.  **Research for simple feedback systems for multiple file reviews:** This was to help me figure out the best way to get multiple people to sign off on graphics, videos and other assets that I would create. A few platforms I tested out and would recommend are [Portals by WeTransfer](https://portals.wetransfer.com/), [Go Visually](https://govisually.com/) and [File Stack](https://www.filestack.com/). However there was already a system in place before I rejoined the team, so I stuck to Slack and Notion and emails for reviews and feedback.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*Iy0lgrpKUqzjBLzDFQ3vsQ.png)

**3. Content strategy:** The next thing I did — after speaking to several people on the team to get more insights on what we were trying to accomplish — was to create content pillars that would guide the **how** and **why** of everything I would design and develop.

**4. Implementation and engaging with more people across the world to get the ball rolling:** The Director of Community and Curation (Helen) and I met up one foggy (on the outside) day in Glasgow and spent about 9am-1pm (in a nice warm coworking space) brainstorming different ways to get the best outcomes. That day, we outlined everything I would do leading up to the campaign week and fixed **deadline dates** for everything (a very important step). We agreed to set up and host meetings with hundreds of verified enterprises in multiple timezones to find more ways to support them and have them network with each other; have the assets and communications/media packs ready by XYZ date; have an external agency write blogs to increase thought leadership; engage more with verified enterprises; divide event tasks for the Social Enterprise World Forum’s Social Enterprise Day event (21 November), etc. All these plans were towards People and Planet First Week (18–22 November). We’re a remote team so meeting in person was great and productive for me. Perhaps my happiness at seeing this Director in-person again pumped my creative thinking and optimism.

**5. Engaging with verified enterprises:** I hosted 2 calls along with the Director of Community, one at about 9am and another at about 9pm to cater to multiple timezones and ensure people were able to join in. When organising campaigns, it’s advisable to always touch base and ensure you’re including primary and secondary stakeholders in all decisions. Let them be aware of what you’re up to, ask them about the help they need to also participate in the campaign, explain what’s in it for them, etc.

After all assets were ready, the next step was to design and mock up what the landing page for People and Planet First Week would look like on Figma, then show it to the team for review before going to the back end to build the actual page on the website, using existing design components and fields that the developer templated. Using Figma allowed me to show everyone what things would look like on the website, and it was easy to implement any feedback they had on Figma, to preview it visually. Once they gave feedback or *no feedback* (which meant all good! on my end), I went on to design and replicate the actual web page design on the website and made it live. The page was a simple landing page and was used to explain the following:

### **4. Communications pack**

Next up I created a communications pack on Notion which housed videos, images, editable templates, social media copy and email texts I created so busy executives and communications teams could easily copy, paste and reuse. The pack was set up with information about People and Planet First Week (18–22 November) as well as Social Enterprise World’s Forum \#SocialEnterpriseDay Event (21 November).

This was to kill 2 birds with one stone as people could share both and participate in both. Now this is an important part of any campaign or event. You need to have a comms pack or media kit to allow people to easily share your campaign assets and tweak them within guided rules and boundaries. For example, suppose you have people who speak Hindi or Spanish and are a major demographic in your event. In that case, you can create a comms pack using Notion, Canva, Google Docs or any other software you find accessible and convenient (Notion and Canva are my go-to). Then use Notion to explain and signpost people on where to download assets, brand guidelines, examples, etc. And use Canva templates to signpost people on how to get and edit certain assets and change copy to Spanish, Hindi and other languages.

## ***Frustrations, lessons and stuff to always remember***

1.  Getting an event into your partners’ and stakeholders’ calendars is one of the best ways to make sure they’re aware of your upcoming events. Send this much ahead of time. However, be cautious about sending so many calendar invites directly to people’s mailboxes if you use Google Calendar. There’s a limit of perhaps 100 to 200 or more depending on the kind of account you have, but Google never fully reveals their limits. If you max this, you and/or your teammates can get shadowbanned from sending calendar invites for a while. Use [www.addevent.com](https://www.addevent.com/) and others.
2.  Need people to take more action? In your newsletters and emails, make sure either the most important news or CTA (call to action) are topmost. Or better still, send an email that only includes one topic and has one call to action. For example, rather than sending a newsletter that asks people to watch a video, download your report, donate at the same time, consider sending an email or newsletter only asking them to download the report. This is one thing Amanda Kiessel from Good Market often advised me to do.
3.  Use mail merge. It’s tough to set up when you have hundreds of emails, but its rewards are sweet to the bones and soul. Why? Because a person’s name is one of the sweetest sounds they’ll ever hear, and when you send an email (not a newsletter) including their names and specific details at specific points, you’re touching sweet spots. I like to call it *Personalisation Pro Max*.
4.  Experiment, try things.
5.  Map out your media and promotional partners ahead of time. Constantly engage with them. Use earned and leveraged media as much as you use paid ads. I am very skilled at building and maintaining relationships and building earned + leveraged media. It’s phenomenal.
6.  Make plans ahead of time if you’re running events but be flexible enough to remember that things don’t always go according to plan. So also ***plan*** for contingencies.

People and Planet First Week was a success! Hundreds of posts flooded social media from verified enterprises as they shared why they decided to get People and Planet First verified, and how they were doing business differently.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*eQX0tU1nrLXymRO7tisL_Q.png)

By the end of the third month and having worked about 54 days by then, for monitoring and evaluation purposes I analysed past performances and analytics; the average open rate of all the newsletters I’d designed and delivered (7 in total) **was over 75%** ([almost twice the marketing benchmark in the nonprofit industry](https://www.brevo.com/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks/)) and my average clickthrough rate for emails was **circa 5 times above the industry average** likewise. This meant people were opening the emails sent, and they were clicking and taking action.

So remember to keep emails simple, and straight to the point. Understand your subscribers and the frequency they may want emails sent to them. Maintain a style too so people know what to expect.

**What is:** [People and Planet First](https://peopleandplanetfirst.org/) is a global collective that was established to verify if an enterprise *actually puts people and planet first before profit while* addressing critical social and/or environmental challenges (not greenwashing about it). Once an enterprise is verified, People and Planet First then supports these enterprises (Fair Trade Enterprise, Social Business, Social Enterprise — they go by many names across the world) to meet some of their growth requirements, by giving them access to pro bono or discounted products and services, networks and much more. There’s something called the *‘People and Planet First verification badge’*, and once an enterprise has met all the criteria, goes through the verification process and receives its certificate, it also gets access to this badge to showcase on its products and websites. The badge assures customers and partners of a business’s genuine commitment to sustainable impact and its commitment to reshaping an economy that benefits people and the planet.

***Pro tip simplified: When you’re leading a campaign, make things simple, be flexible, and always have a backup plan. Oh, and also have water or any other energy drink you’ve got! Have great teammates too.***
