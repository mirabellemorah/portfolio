If this is the question, as it was the question when Hameedah and [Ummulikhaeri Imam-Lawal](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ummulikhaeri-imam-lawal-/) invited me to speak about “*Creative Arts & Media: Digital Content Creation, animation, Graphic Design, and Film

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*MIPWU7Ecr6Gs_3BdqfXwCQ.jpeg)

The creative space is vast, and in the topic given to me, the only two I could focus on were digital content creation and animation, as well as my journey into it, which was in no way straightforward.

## Goodness, we need good school counsellors!

I wish I had a better career counsellor in secondary school. I just knew I was going to be in the Arts and Commercial division/class during senior secondary school (so you see during my time in Nigeria, in senior secondary school you get divided into about two groups: you’re either a science student or you’re an art/commercial student. Both groups of students get divided into very separate classes and often don’t take many of the same subjects, except perhaps for biology, geography and a few others that cut across the board.)

As I was saying, a counsellor could have helped my dilemma better.

What should I study at university? What am I good at?

That was a question I didn’t fully know the answer to. I thought I wanted economics, especially if it made me money.

One thing led to another and (laughs) I used my very own hands with God’s help and got myself into the English and Literary Studies Department at the university. And that was the story of my becoming.

## **But seriously, what advice would I give about the creative world to a recent secondary school graduate?**

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1200/1*G9QZGVUYCG5fB_YJlswcLg.png)

### 1. Grow confident

I can’t tell you what decisions to make, or where a path could ultimately lead you to (though I can give you a hint). But in whatever you choose to explore, make sure you **grow your confidence**. How? Ah well, I wish I had a magic pill myself, but I guess you’ve just got to do these things that scare you — no no not the negative stuff! Just positive stuff that scares you. Like going to competitions. Talking to people you want to talk to. Attending seminars and conferences that you feel are out of your league (but who’s to say if it’s out of your league or not?), and you’ve got a keen interest in being part of it and learning from it.

Read tons of books. Learn to speak in public. Enroll in fellowships or clubs that give you opportunities to speak to small crowds. Start with sharing a piece of information with 3–10 people. Then grow into leading conversations with more people. Volunteer to do things. Just do the hard things and explore different domains of knowledge. Do things very well, give it your best shot even if it doesn’t work at first. It’ll be embarrassing at the start when you’re a beginner at things. You’ll make mistakes but seriously *fam*. Would your future self really care that much? Nobody is thinking that much about you — except your parents perhaps.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*c3CFAgYblEB37mibTc0ZmQ.jpeg)

### 2. Feed your mind

You’ll thank yourself for this. You become what you see. There’s often a gap between what your mind feels like it can create and what your hands end up creating. Trust me I know this. In the design world, you need to constantly feed your mind and eyes with designs, with other artists’ works. It inspires you, fuels you, you get to explore multiple styles and this helps you ultimately discover your own style (if it ever happens).

Read about the failures of other people too, not just success stories. Study other fields of life, not just your core interests. You’ll be able to piece dots together and play with different concepts. See failure as a stepping stone.

See failure as successfully figuring out what doesn’t work. *Yay!*

I know your grades in school may have taught you that in life, there are only failures and successes, and no middle ground. Well. Sometimes, there are grey areas in life. Middle grounds. It’s neither good nor bad, just a weird limbo. But remember, you can always rise up from what you perceive as a ‘failure’. Ask for help too and ask questions. There are many people willing to help you, sometimes we don’t just know who they are yet.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1200/1*j-3ERnugvRV6G8eBPu36Xg.png)

### 3. Money

If you’re on the entrepreneurial side, focus on what you can give first for great value precedes great wealth. Understand finances, and learn to negotiate. When you master a skill, demand for what you believe you’re worth. Study. Go online and find out what others in your field are earning. However, my personal opinion about this is that you must first attain a certain level of understanding and mastery of your work, delivering great, perceived and actual value, if you must have returning customers and more referrals. Volunteering and interning will often give you a learning pathway and often earnings at the same time. Be a good person.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1200/1*LJGkinu_XGbE0lBFL-15QQ.png)

### 4. Be patient

Just be patient. Getting headaches? Getting frustrated? Leave it, get some sleep and come back to attack the task again with a fresh head. It quite often works. But be patient. After many hours, days, weeks, and years of building your craft, consistently and persistently, one day, you’ll wake up and discover you’ve become a god at what you do. Then they’ll say hey! S/he’s an overnight success. Wow!

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*xR99BImiwVh0IKL8Rtc3iA.gif)

### 5. Use YouTube

YouTube will teach you a lot of things at the start of your creative career. So use it well. It’s free, you’ve only got to, sadly, pay with a few seconds of watching tons of ads. But YouTube is a fine resource.

So to you reading this, on your journey, whatever you choose to do, I wish you a very good success!
