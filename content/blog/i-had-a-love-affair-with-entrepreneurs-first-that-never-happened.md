I had a love affair with Entrepreneurs First that never happened.

I filled out the application. I made the cut for an interview, and after the interview, the next day, maybe less than 24 hours? I was told I wasn’t eligible. Actually, on the day of the interview itself, which was just 15 minutes sweet, I was told I wasn’t eligible. That’s the love affair that never happened.

During my interview, I had said that I’d already started a business, [Grohwie](https://www.grohwie.com) that it was profitable and I was generating revenue. Welp, as soon as I said that, my interviewer told me,

> “*Hey, but I think we’re too late for you because you’re already building something.*”

To be honest, that kind of put me off track of what I was saying. I immediately said back,

> *“Okay, great, but what if I came on board having already proven that I can generate revenue? Everyone agrees that I’m very good on the business side of things. You’re looking for someone who’s technical, so it’s always a blend between technical skill sets and business skill sets.”*

While I thought I had gotten back on track, I was asked questions like, **“In the master’s degree you did in entrepreneurship and innovation, what made you stand out from your colleagues? What made you different?”** I explained that I felt everyone was unique in their own sense, and while many people did go for formal jobs, I was one of the few people who solely went to risk it all in entrepreneurship. I’ve been doing this for over a year, just generating revenue, cold emailing thousands of people, finding out how to make people need more of what they want (*you dig?)*.

I was also asked, “*What do you look for, or what do you need to have, in a co-founder? What’s a challenge you’ve had that you’re trying to solve at the moment?*” I asked if she meant an area in business or in my personal life. And she said yeah, whichever I chose.

It was one of the quickest interviews I’ve ever had. Rapid on the spot thinking. Fifteen minutes, but those 15 minutes impacted a whole lot. She asked me so many questions, about four or five.

So I mentioned it to an incredible founder who’s also an EF alumni and was my first client, I’d call him A. I told A about my rejection and he said:

> “It doesn’t sound like a rejection from EF, just a not now. Their mission is entrepreneurship so they step aside for people already doing it. That door will still be open in the future.”

Loved it! So yeah, it was a love affair because aside liking the branding, I loved the mission and vision of this. They say if you love something, you let it go. So I let that go and continued within my accelerator programs with [Foundervine/NatWest (Royal Bank of Scotland)](https://www.foundervine.com/our-programmes/the-scale-circle), Converge Challenge which I’d made progressed unto the pitching rounds, as part of the University of Edinburgh contingent under the Create Change Challenge category.
