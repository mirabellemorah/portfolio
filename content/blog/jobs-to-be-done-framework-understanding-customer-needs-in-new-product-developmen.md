***Jobs to be done framework***

**I’m done with reading Anthony Ulwick’s book: Jobs to Be Done: Theory to Practice.**

As of the moment writing this, I love this framework and its potential to get you closer to a user’s mind and real needs, yet I am still confused by these 2 things:

1.  What’s the difference between the job a user is hiring a product to do? Vs the solutions the user wants?
2.  Why can’t I just ask the user in a survey “what job are you hiring this product to do?”

The book, from my understanding, says we can’t always do the latter because users/customers may not always know what job they’re hiring a product or service to do (or perhaps they have several jobs and needs they want met with one product).

Now, what questions should I then be asking that would guarantee I discover these jobs and inner motivations?

**Pizza example:**

- Possible job: I am hiring this pizza 🍕from Dominos to stop my hunger.
- Possible outcome: so I don’t die of starvation?

**Looking at the framework**

I understand that this is an outcome-based framework. Rather than just building ideas and praying that people catch up to the idea (build and they’ll come), the JTBD framework instead focuses on a more practical approach, which is to understand the outcomes a user wants to get. This makes a product more successful and allows a company to understand the different outcomes users are looking for and, then understand and/or create segments based on underserved or over-served needs.  
  
**Theory to practice**

Even though I don’t understand this framework and theory fully yet, I’ve made my own attempts to implement it.  
  
In a recent application and in surveying early career designers in Nigeria who want to be part of a design fellowship, I asked the following questions:  
  
a. what specific challenge or barrier currently holds you back in your design journey?

b\. how do you hope this fellowship will help overcome it?

c\. why is solving this challenge important to you right now

d\. and what impact will it have on your future as a designer?

The desired outcome on my own end is to fully understand their needs and tailor the fellowship’s curriculum based on their own needs. So I’m hoping this matches up to the JTBD framework, in my desire to understand their needs better and how the fellowship’s solution matches that.
