There’s a concept that I like to call the **marathonization of life** and I’ll explain it.

> You run like a marathon, you know, or like a relay. No matter how long it takes you, at least you’re moving towards a project or an idea. It’s better than waiting for the perfect sprint, of which many times the perfect time never really comes.

For about a year or two I’d been struggling to read a certain book called “Go Givers Sell More” (I think that’s the name).

I realized that the more I said I would read it, the more I didn’t read it because I had compounding interests taking over my time. One day I had a conversation with a friend. It wasn’t really a smooth conversation but it was a conversation that made me question myself a lot and the activities that I did. I admired in my friend that she was very quick in the things that she did but for me it took a lot of time to do those things. I would sometimes beat myself up for not being that fast, even though I would complete them. She made me think about how we all have different patterns of execution and how we all get to the same destination but with different strategies. I then decided to run marathons with some of my activities.

Rather than saying I wanted to accomplish something in a sprint and waiting for the perfect time to begin that sprint, I instead just thought, “You know what, it already took me one or two years to actually start reading this book so why don’t I right now just start reading it every day, anyway, a chapter a day? Even if it takes me three months, at least I began something,” and that’s what I refer to as the marathonization of life.

You run like a marathon, you know, or like a relay. No matter how long it takes you, at least you’re moving towards a project or an idea. It’s better than waiting for the perfect sprint, of which many times the perfect time never really comes.
