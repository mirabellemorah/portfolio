## What is marketing?

First of all marketing is all about the different activities that you, your company, and other organisations undertake to spread the word about your products and services so they can make more money — or achieve any other KPIs / goals they’ve got.

## And traditional marketing?

These are some of the earliest techniques people use to promote their businesses. That is, they use offline methods to reach their target audience. Think newspaper, magazine, TV, and radio advertisements (Source: [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/traditional-marketing/#:~:text=Traditional%20marketing%20is%20any%20form,and%20TV%20and%20radio%20advertisements.)). These methods still work, depending on the target audience and what’s being sold.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*AyLwweQ5bjmM543r)

The marketing mix, often called the four Ps of marketing — product, price, place, and promotion — is the foundation of any marketing strategy. These elements are the building blocks that businesses use to plan and market their products or services, and they all work together to achieve success. So whether you’re a traditional marketer, a digital marketer, or a growth hacker, these 4 Ps apply to you.

## Knock, Knock — Who’s There? The Growth Hacker!

A growth hacker doesn’t get pulled in all directions. They focus on GROWTH and the few significant things that bring it. As [Sean Ellis](https://www.linkedin.com/article/edit/7219384262876102656/#) says, “A growth hacker is a person whose true north is growth.”

> *“Growth hacking is when you combine marketing, psychology, and technology to achieve business results and growth,” says Bronson Taylor.*

While traditional and digital marketers worry about various things like logo design, the look of a landing page, and managing external vendors, a growth hacker cares only about things that lead to growth. Growth can mean different things for different businesses — more users, beta testers, or money. Think of a growth hacker as a scientist who experiments with many things to see what works best and what to discard.

## How I added 600 people to my email list

For example, when building BlankPaperz, one of the quickest ways I got email signups was when I did the Writers Contest and got over 600 entries in barely 2 weeks. The good side of this was that:

1.  Participants agreed to join our newsletter.
2.  We automatically had 600 new email signups (whoop whoop!).
3.  I learned for sure that offering financial incentives could motivate people to enter a competition.

> *That was not an excellent psychological calculation and timing, which I would not be repeating*

A downside was that several people who were now new to the email list may not have been in the best alignment with BlankPaperz’s mission of literary leadership and amplifying the voices of African changemakers.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*YzsOFpzduJ4FKd6h)

Also, I sent out “unfortunately, we didn’t select you as a winner” emails to people early in the morning and at the start of a new month. That was not an excellent psychological calculation and timing, which I would not be repeating. I now believe it is best to find some time during the day and on a date when my target audience does not ascribe any significant or cultural meaning to. The target country for the contest was Nigeria, and many Nigerians like to keep the first few days of a new month “sacred”. They pray for good luck for the rest of the month. So, a sad message on the first few days of a new month can demoralise some people for the next few weeks.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*DTJzToOCbcSmk1nX)

## 3 excellent qualities a growth hacker should possess

Understanding cultural nuances is essential for a growth hacker. Someone growing a product or service for a business must:

1.  have a burning desire to understand the target market
2.  find creative ways to connect people with the business solutions and ideas their company offers.
3.  keep testing new techniques plus evolving existing strategies that they’ve figured out, or that other organisations have proven to work.
