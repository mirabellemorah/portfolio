What is it exactly? Secrecy or something else?

When researching and writing about Nigerian tech startups, I believe there's a lack of transparency and limited public documentation about certain operational and financial details. When I received a grant from the Mastercard Foundation and Edinburgh Innovations to further develop Grohwie Design, a friend advised me not to share the press release on my WhatsApp status to avoid people asking for money. She emphasized the importance of keeping the grant amount private.


While I understand her concern, it made me think of more logical reasons why many startups in Nigeria do not disclose information about the funds they’ve raised and other financial and operational details. Despite my extensive research and writing on Nigerian and African startups over the past few months, I've found it challenging to find relevant online data about the funds many startups have raised and other crucial information to support my research. In contrast, when I've researched and written about European startups, I've found abundant data on their inception, operations, and publicly available financial accounts. Much of this data is very valuable for analyzing and using a startup as a case study.

Because I had difficulties finding online data about many Nigerian startups, I decided to reach out to a co-founder of a prominent fintech company to prepare an academic case study and presentation at the University of Edinburgh, on the innovativeness of their products. I wanted insights into their innovative strategies and to ensure I included accurate information about the startup.

So I reached out to the co-founder and ... crickets.

I suggest that startups should include regular public reports in their annual communications plan. Making reports and relevant information publicly accessible is essential. It is challenging to contribute meaningfully to existing research about African or Nigerian startups if there's limited publicly available data and information about these businesses’ operations.

The next logical step for a researcher to gather data is directly contacting startups. However, this approach has its own challenges, such as timing, obtaining contact details, and waiting for responses from some founders.
