Let’s reduce the technical bits of building a useful website that shows you’ve been solving some social and/or environmental problems for people.

## Cool + useful stuff to hang on your organisation’s website

Your digital presence should reflect your organization’s values and mission:

1.  **Use impact numbers:** Show the output (stuff you did) and outcomes (results from the stuff you did) of your events, outreaches, etc.
2.  **Touch my heart and hopefully my pockets:** share beneficiary stories and success cases.
3.  **Are you trustworthy enough for my money?:** if you’ve received funds, consider creating public versions of certain impact reports to demonstrate how funds were used for certain programmes.
4.  **Now that we are here please tell us what next to do:** make it easy for people to support your cause. Always add calls to action. They can be donation buttons, a dedicated page on how to get involved or external links at the end of every blog you write.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Just as your social enterprise works to create inclusive change, your website should be accessible to many people:

1.  **Good speedy connection**: Keep your image resolution low, and clear cached files when needed so your website loads quicker and people can access your content whether they’re in Sambisa or Toronto.
2.  **Multiple languages:** Consider plugins or addons that can switch to multiple languages.
3.  **Think of the people visiting your website from their mobile phones:** A lot of people view websites on mobile, so make sure your website is working very well, and the design is well formatted for mobile view.

## Practical steps forward

If you’ve already got a website

1.  **Check that the mission statement you’ve stated on your website is clear**.
2.  Let your purpose immediately be clear once someone reads it.
3.  Highlight your unique approach in it. “Our mission is to help 20,000 kids in Lanu get to school by building special scooters for them… etc”.
4.  **Demonstrate your impact by sharing success stories as case studies.**
5.  Display key metrics and numbers you’ve achieved.
6.  Use infographics and visuals (could be a downloadable report on your website or a page detailing your impact).
7.  **Always tell people what next they can do.**
8.  Make your donation button easy to find and easy to use. It could be positioned in your nav/menu bar, in your footer, or as a sticky feature that constantly sticks to a point in your website no matter how much people scroll.
9.  Volunteer sign-up forms should be easy to find on a “how to get involved page”.
10. Consider building up your newsletter subscriptions. Try [Substack](https://on.substack.com/about), [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/), [Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/) or [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/).
11. **If you’ve got social media accounts**, include the links on your website

## Finally

The most effective social enterprise websites combine compelling storytelling with clear paths to action. They don’t just inform — they inspire, engage, and tell people how they can get involved. In a world where digital presence can multiply your impact, can your mission afford to stay offline?

Whether you’re fighting poverty, promoting sustainability, or advancing education, your online presence could be the difference between reaching hundreds or millions with your message.

Make every click count for good!
