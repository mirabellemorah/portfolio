I’ve always wanted to understand how projection mapping works, especially as someone who creates motion designs. So I attended a two-day program called ImmersiveLabs, organized by Surge and Scottish Youth Theatre.

To be honest, the night before the first day I seriously contemplated whether to go at all. I was kind of torn between working that weekend to pay my bills and attending the workshop. But I REAALLYY wanted to understand how immersive, mixed, and interactive media and technologies worked for the longest time, and apparently it was a very competitive program, because there were only about 15 slots open, and I had landed one of them. Mhmm, now I had a real tension between creative growth and survival.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1200/1*pI5bMEv-abgv1TAj6IZntg.jpeg)

It made me think about why, at a good number of the creative events I’ve attended in Glasgow, I rarely see many Black people in the room. Sometimes I’m the only Black person there. Occasionally there are two or three of us, sometimes four (if I’m very lucky). But that’s a conversation for another day, but I suspect people are often just trying to earn their daily bread and don’t have enough time to pursue some of the other things happening in the creative world.

## ImmersiveLabs

On the first day of the ImmersiveLabs workshop, I decided to go, because it was too great an opportunity to miss. The first day was about exploring different types of immersive technologies, from chatbots to augmented reality to VR headsets and much more. I was happy but exhausted by 4pm.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*50EgvBjvolZcfaQ-QfdwDw.jpeg)

The second day had us working on group projects. My team and I created a haunted experience based on the idea of a baby seal that had been kidnapped, with its mother coming after it.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*GHNvxcn4poipj-DFcfh36Q.jpeg)

We built a blacked-out room with props and used a VR headset to bring visitors into our haunted space. Wearing the headset, our visitors would see the mother (or monster) emerging from a red sea, and they could also speak to the kidnapped baby seal through a chatbot we’d created, which would respond to them. We borrowed the idea from the Selkie myth. I worked with another team member to build out the character for our seal and create an interactivechatbot.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*b8EGyX5ntUGTCTWUwkd6pg.jpeg)

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*FI_bUsf-YxZbovPpOcm8iw.jpeg)

I also helped animate some of the work that was projected, which is where the projection mapping came in.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*_wVUR815blGlzb7XGzHeUg.jpeg)

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*s_ywc6VWhn6-6X_qP-pvlg.jpeg)

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*OB2wAz8HPNXH6AbzrfQaqg.jpeg)

The next day, I went home, bought myself a small projector, and started experimenting with projection mapping. I already had a VR headset that I hadn’t used much. Honestly, the program was incredibly exciting and opened my mind to things I’d assumed were much harder than they actually are. I’m looking forward to what I’ll create next.
