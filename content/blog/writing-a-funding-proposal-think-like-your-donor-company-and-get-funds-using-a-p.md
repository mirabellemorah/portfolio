***“…you’re no longer just another funding request. You’re a strategic partner they can’t afford to ignore.”***

*“A PESTEL analysis is an acronym for a tool used to identify the macro (external) forces facing an organisation. The letters stand for Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental and Legal.” — *[*Oxford College of Marketing*](https://blog.oxfordcollegeofmarketing.com/2016/06/30/pestel-analysis/)

Imagine you’re at a cocktail party, trying to impress a potential investor. You wouldn’t just launch into a dry monologue about your brilliant business plan, would you? No, you’d subtly weave in shared interests. You’d mention your mutual love for vintage vinyl, your admiration for their recent philanthropic endeavors, or your insightful commentary on the latest political scandal (with a dash of humor, of course). By demonstrating genuine interest and understanding of their world, you build rapport and increase your chances of securing that crucial investment. A PESTEL analysis helps you do the same with funding proposals. It allows you to show your donor that you’re not just another charity seeking a handout. You’re a strategic partner who understands their challenges, aligns with their values, and offers a solution that genuinely benefits their organization.

When preparing communications documents or funding proposals to ask for external funding, use a PESTEL analysis framework to align your proposal with your donor’s interests and concerns. When you consider the political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal factors that impact your donor, you can tailor your project and solutions to resonate with their priorities and challenges. For instance, if a donor company is heavily invested in environmental sustainability, the document you’re preparing to send out should highlight how the proposed project aligns with environmental sustainability.

It’s not always about what you want, it’s about showing how your project speaks directly to their world.

Again solve problems from your donor’s perspective: What political trends might they care about? Or are they in political trouble? If they’re in political trouble and you bring a project which has heavy political elements, this might raise a red flag for them to not get deeper involved. Or it might be a green flag for them to use your project and redeem their political image. Context matters.

By weaving these insights into your communications, you’re not just asking for money — you’re offering a solution that feels tailor-made for their specific challenges.

So don’t just list facts. Tell a story that connects your project’s potential to your donor’s broader organisational objectives. Show them you’ve done your homework and understand their ecosystem. When donors see that you’ve thought through how your initiative fits into their bigger picture, you’re no longer just another funding request. You’re a strategic partner they can’t afford to ignore.

Now go forth and charm those donors!
