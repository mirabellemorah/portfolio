## Summary of some of the things I wish I did better

**A timeline of my entrepreneurial and change-making activities**

_9 things doing a masters in a prestigious business school didn't teach me and my suggestions to fellow entrepreneurs_

I started [BlankPaperz](https://www.blankpaperz.com) when I was 17 and I had no idea what I was doing or where I was going. I also made up a lot of things along the way. I watched YouTube so much and when I didn’t have money to pay for courses, I watched every single free course I could find on Udemy etc. because I wanted to learn and keep improving myself. The majority of the time, I didn't have the money to pay people to do things like build a website or design graphics, so I learned to do them myself.

I built BlankPaperz website by watching a lot of YouTube videos. My initial idea was to publish stories of young changemakers online and encourage young creative writers in Nigeria and as many African countries as I could get to send in writing submissions about issues they were facing in society. My first web host, if I remember right, was WhoGoHost, and I used the smallest and cheapest hosting plan I could find. But when my website slowed, I kept upgrading the hosting plan. Slow meant that my traffic was growing.

**Some events could have been better**

I disliked one of the very first events I hosted because I wish I could have done it better. I spent less than N7000 on that event and had close to 70 people or more sign up to attend it. I learned that I could have provided food or snacks for people and capped the number of people who attended (they were a lot for the space, but that was a great problem to have). In subsequent events I've organised, I always have an attendance limit, and I know how to get my audience to sign up. I've also learned a lot being part of the core team of the Social Enterprise World Forum and seen how events with thousands of participants have been excellently put in place across multiple countries.

### A brief timeline of my journey

**Age 17 - 19:** Started and grew BlankPaperz Media

**Age 20:** BlankPaperz was already recognised by international organisations like Ashoka and We Are Family Foundation and by the US and British Governments for our contributions to education and social leadership.

**Age 21:** Freelanced my way into being a Digital Productions and Marketing Consultant for Stand.Earth, a North American climate campaign movement.

**Age 22:** Began my role as Community and Communications Manager for the Social Enterprise World Forum.

**Age 23:** Began GC Funds arm of BlankPaperz to support formal, creative and entrepreneurial education-based needs of individuals and organisations in Nigeria.

**Age 25:** BlankPaperz had grown to include an e-magazine, hosted multiple events, featured over 200 contributing writers, reached over 100k web users, and provided N1.7 million in educational support through GC Fund. Finally, I began my MSc in Entrepreneurship and Innovation at the University of Edinburgh’s Business School as a Mastercard Foundation Scholar. Because I needed to deepen my knowledge about the business aspect of many things I was just "winging" and shooting several (successful) shots at. We can also say the Holy Spirit was directing me.

### Things I didn’t need to learn in business school as a young entrepreneur

1. Persistence Pays Off: Your 17th idea might be the one that works, not your first.
People Buy from People They Like: To get people to buy from you, they need to feel positive towards you, not just your product or service.

2. Reduce Uncertainty: You need to reduce people’s uncertainty and fear of several unknown things about your product or service. You’ll hesitate about buying something if you aren’t sure your choice is wise, won’t you? So, find ways to reassure people and let them know they’re making wise decisions.

3. Smiles Don't Equal Sales: People can be friendly, but that doesn't mean they'll give you money.

4. The Power of Perception: "Expensive = good" is a powerful psychological tactic that works. That's one reason why luxury products sell. Expense also = prestige and social currency.

5. Master the Basics: Learn to manage your cash flow, balance sheet, and profit and loss statements.

6. People Are Your Business: Always remember that you’re in the ‘people business.’ People will buy from you, people will attend your events – not sticks. People will work with and for you. So understand people and treat people well.

7. Skin in the Game: Want people to take on more tasks and responsibility? Let them have more skin in the game. 

8. Delegate tasks; sometimes, people disappoint you, but others will impress you.

9. Teamwork na Baba: You can’t do it alone, at least not if you want to expand.
