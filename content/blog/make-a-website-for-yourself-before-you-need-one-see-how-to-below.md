There are thousands of people as skilled and as talented as yourself; people who have missed out on finger-licking global opportunities and fellowships, because while someone who knew their work and worth was going to recommend them for an opportunity, there was little to no public record — a LinkedIn page, an Instagram account or even a website — to show proof to the other body, that the person had done what they were being nominated for.

There are many other reasons why you need a personal website as a changemaker, a social entrepreneur or someone who plans to be in the public eye.

In the presence of sudden fortune, we don’t rise to lofty ideals, instead, we fall to our level of preparation. Make one before you need one.

## Where and how to lead more people to your body of work (where to build a website)

1.  **About.me:** If you enjoy quick easy peasy things, try [about.me](http://about.me). It’s free and fast; not exactly a ‘website’, but it works as a website. I made my [about.me page as a teenager](https://about.me/mirabellemorah), telling everyone who cared to visit it that I was “a student of English and Literary Studies, singer and a guitarist. I volunteer in my spare time as well. And I believe that ‘to utter, is to alter.’ — Niyi Osundare.”
2.  **Use LinkTree:** [That one’s free too](https://linktr.ee/). While it won’t allow you to write a full epistle on who you are, you can add multiple links to important places you want people to find you — from LinkedIn to blog posts you’ve published.
3.  **Super.so:** How about building a website that looks as cute as Notion? Something that isn’t complex? [Try Super](https://super.so/) especially if you already [use Notion](https://www.notion.so/) as a project management tool.
4.  **Wix**: [Wix](http://wix.com) lets you build good and really nice websites too. The learning curve isn’t too steep and there are templates to start you out with.
5.  **Elementor**: It lets you build [really cool websites](https://elementor.com/) with drag-and-drop features, and lets you manage your content via WordPress too. It’s like a double-edged — not sword but — candy. Hosting and content management system in one place with many plugins that allow you to include e-commerce stores, Google Analytics, and a myriad of flexible add-ons
6.  **Webflow and Framer**: Just check out Webflow’s [directory of cute websites.](https://webflow.com/made-in-webflow/directory) Delish! And [Framer](https://webflow.com/made-in-webflow/directory)’s too. Finger licking! But along with the ‘ooh laa las’ come steep learning curves with these two. They can be difficult to pull off and would require a good knowledge of designing skills and I would say, even coding functionalities.

Others include [Squarespace](https://www.squarespace.com/) and [Google Sites.](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/9310491?hl=en#sites_create_name) If you also buy a domain name with GoDaddy, you should try their [website builder.](https://www.godaddy.com/en-uk/websites/website-builder) It’s simple drag and drop and they make it so easy to use.

Get a website before you need one. Document your work there.

## Example

Tracy is an incredible young woman that I know. In fact, she’s a champ! She founded ATTA Breastmilk Community in Uganda to create a safe, inclusive system for mothers to donate breastmilk to support fragile newborns. I like Tracy’s work a lot, so while testing out and learning to code and develop websites, I created [a simple website about Tracy](https://tracyahumuza.superhi.hosting/) on SuperHi.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*Vjk_bqZXFx8lHnPODYTSog.gif)

It simply included a link to her Instagram page and told an inspiring story about why she does the work she does with ATTA. Then very importantly links back to ATTA’s website.
