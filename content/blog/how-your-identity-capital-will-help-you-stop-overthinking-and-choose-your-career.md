According to Gemini, your identity capital is what sociologists describe as all the experiences, skills, hobbies, and resources that you’ve accumulated over time. You can look at it as a personal brand. Meg Jay, a clinical psychologist, has made the concept of “identity capital” really popular in her book [“The Defining Decade: Why Your Twenties Matter”.](https://amzn.eu/d/aXExN7G)

## How do you earn identity capital?

Below is an excerpt from “The Defining Decade: Why Your Twenties Matter.” According to Meg, it will help you decide on the next best thing to take on.

*“You earn identity capital along the way. It is our stock of personal assets. It is how we add value to who we are and it is what we have to show for how we have spent our time. These are the investments we make in ourselves, the things we do well enough or long enough that they become part of who we are. Identity capital is how we build ourselves bit by bit over time. It can be personal, where we come from, how we solve problems.*

*A culmination of our unique identity capital is what we bring to the adult marketplace and it is the currency we use to “purchase“ jobs and opportunities we want. 20-somethings who take time to explore and also have the nerve to make commitments along the way have stronger identities. They have higher self-esteem, more perseverance, a clearer sense of self, resistance to conformity, and more life satisfaction. More often than not, life opportunities and careers are made not out of college degrees and GPAs but out of a couple of door-opening pieces of identity capital.*

***So take the job with the most identity capital.*** *You can’t think your way through life and the only way to figure out what to do is to just do something.”*
