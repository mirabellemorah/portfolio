On my most recent visit to Kano, Nigeria, I facilitated a session with several communications managers and leaders from multiple NGOs, on ‘*entrepreneurial and innovative thinking especially for awesome communications managers’.* I was in the process of completing my MSc in Entrepreneurship and Innovation from Edinburgh University and these are some of the things I shared with them, that I’m now sharing with you. This session was hosted by Bridge Connect Africa Initiative.

## 1. Risk-taking propensities

The algorithm is moving much faster than your shadow. And is that something that’s really within your circle of control?

[According to this BBC article](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/articles/z6dpxyc#:~:text=An%20entrepreneur%20is%20someone%20who,the%20entrepreneurial%20spirit%20for%20us.), “an entrepreneur is someone who starts their own company or business, seeking to make a profit or a difference in the world, or both! ***Entrepreneurs often take risks, creating or pouncing on new opportunities as they arise and they rarely restrict themselves to just one industry.***”

The last part is what I want you as a communications person to focus on the most. Think like you call the shots. What exactly are you trying to achieve for your organisation or company by being on social media? Is it to get more likes? Increase followers? Sell more products? Educate people?

Whatever it is, **know your game**. Know what platform your primary users are on and their demographic as well. Then when you’re running campaigns or making reels and so on, take those risks with the trends. Try multiple content types and see what works. Use a different tone for a week and see if there’s any change. Have a celeb on board or an influencer. Ask a controversial question, etc. I enjoy how [GTBank plays to the ‘funky](https://www.instagram.com/gtbank/) youthful trendy’ content vibe yet also plays to the ‘high-level bank for all’ vibe.

## 2. Define your locus of control

Do your comms team members blame everything on something else other than themselves, or do they take ownership and responsibility not only for things that went well but things that burnt and went down the drain too? The two loci of control include:

- **Internal locus of control:** you are the author of your fortunes and misfortunes. You take responsibility for all your actions and their outcomes.
- **External locus of control:** fate intervenes here. You tend to see things as not your fault and not your responsibility, but rather as a consequence of external factors.

An internal locus of control is a mindset skill I’d recommend the most for comms people to develop, so they can tackle and find solutions to emerging and existing communications challenges as they arise. I must also say that this does not mean you should take responsibility for things that are obviously not your fault. But rather there should be a healthy balance, with the most leaning towards an internal locus of control.

## 3. Understand priorities and manage an expanding task list

In a recent survey on the top [10 communications challenges](https://www.swordandthescript.com/2022/08/communications-challenges/) comms managers faced, 31% said they had way too many priorities and it was difficult getting to the important things with too many priorities. For dealing with an ever-expanding task list, I recommend:

1.  Setting up a communications plan or strategy to guide you through a quarter, or at best a year.
2.  Involving your team members and letting them know your comms plans and capacity.
3.  Breaking down every activity into subtasks that need to be fulfilled for that activity to be completed. That way you’ll easily find your time thieves.
4.  And declining or deprioritising tasks that are not important and not urgent. This gives you space to dig into more important tasks.

## 4. Create partnerships + actively listen to everything and everyone

Build partnerships and collaborations not just within the team, but with external media partners, promotional partners, other companies, etc. Ask your users for feedback, take note of the complaints and praises of the people who review your product or service on Reddit and other anonymous channels.

## 5. Innovative thinking

Communications managers should be innovative thinkers. Innovation can simply be incremental in nature where you’re not building anything uniquely new, but rather taking an existing product or service or content or idea, and then making it better. You can use the intel you’ve gained from actively listening to users’ pain points, and improve on what already exists. For example is there a competitor who writes blogs in a certain way and has more attention? But a lot of people while they enjoy the information, complain about the use of profanity in the blogs. An incremental case of innovation, in the context of blog writing, would be to use your brand tone, inculcate some of the strategies competitor A uses and then rather than include profanities, include words of motivation, links to other resources, or something more uplifting.

## 6. Lastly

Question everything, be a learner and a doer.
