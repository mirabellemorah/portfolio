This was part of the website I coded and developed with 3 other team members during Code First Girls’ Kickstarter course on Intro to Web Development. It was our assignment. Our *‘boss mode*’ final project. It was not a perfect website. It was a beginner’s first try at something; the start of a great, inspiring and impactful journey in web development.

> “I think Bey would be proud”

## My role

- I designed the large hero section ([the first thing you see on the page](https://mirabellemorah.github.io/beyonce-CFG/)).
- I used JavaScript to design the “listen” button to allow for a smooth scrolling experience towards Beyonce’s past albums.
- Worked on the album cards to make them responsive.
- Added animations to the website using gradients and hover effects (CSS).
- On the final day for presentations, I presented on behalf of the entire group, speaking about the functionalities we created and why we made certain choices.

**There were 4 of us in this group:** Audrey, Indigo, Rachel and myself. Note that for many of us, **this was one of the first times we would be working with codes.** And choosing Beyonce, was a random selection we were all okay working on.

## Joys and frustrations

Oh like everything new, my neural pathways were still forming and developing, so lots were giving me headaches. Code First Girls’ [Coding Kickstarter Introduction to Web Development](https://codefirstgirls.arlo.co/w/courses/215-coding-kickstarter-remote-introduction-to-web-development) was also an 8-week, remote course.

It was around the same time that I was deep into my research dissertation for my MSc in Entrepreneurship and Innovation at the University of Edinburgh. I was [building Grohwie](https://codefirstgirls.arlo.co/w/courses/215-coding-kickstarter-remote-introduction-to-web-development) as part of my dissertation, a platform to help designers, especially within the social impact space. During that time as well, I was travelling between the UK, Nigeria, South Africa and back again to the UK, researching, [facilitating communications workshops](https://www.instagram.com/p/C90GRwuxaPQ/) for comms leaders from NGOs in Northern Nigeria, [speaking at events](https://www.instagram.com/p/C-X7CNSPVi-/?img_index=1), [collecting data across over 6 cities](https://www.instagram.com/p/C9fgezDgPKx/) and still trying to attend every single virtual CFG class.

I fell sick many times.

One thing I wished the CFG facilitator did better was to explain the rationale behind the use of certain lines of code. Though 2 hours a week for virtual lessons can be rough, yet I would have loved to have more detailed explanations on why she was using a line of code over another, or what the code functions meant, and so so on, rather than me following on some occasions and simply mirroring/writing lines of code that I do not fully understand what they mean. Good thing some of us mentioned during the classes that we would appreciate more explanations, in addition to our personal, after-class research.

I need to understand the rules and reasons behind things, so I can *innovatively* break these rules later on to do more creative things. But more practically, so I can understand what and how to troubleshoot and fix bugs and challenges when they come up.

## Tools that helped me

[Mimo.org](http://mimo.org) was an incredibly exceptional tool and coding companion in my journey to learning the basics, meaning and theory behind code snippets. Mimo is a coding software that is gamified and teaches you how to code. Think of it as Duolingo for coding.

## Doing social good with web development

The more I learned, I decided to develop beta websites for my friends who were doing incredible work in society, making where we live much better.

I started by making [a beta website](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mirabellemorah_it-just-so-happened-that-as-i-was-building-activity-7269417589821300736-LPhW?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop) on SuperHi for Damilola Onafuwa who is a documentary filmmaker and photographer.
