This is something that I’ve had to learn especially when starting a business as a creative. This is for you if you’re going the business side as a creative. Maybe you want to start out as a freelancer and then end up running a studio or an agency.

There are three types/levels of creative business ownership:

**1. The technician level:** The technician is a person who has the skill sets and knows how to do everything themselves. You do not delegate the work to people, you take on everything. This is why many times you hear freelancers saying that they cannot take on more than 1–3 jobs at a time. Nothing wrong per se if a person’s preference is this phase.

**2. The manager level:** Now at this stage you have transitioned from just being the person with the skill sets to now being the person who has someone else that you are maybe subcontracting to or employing. The issue here is that sometimes managers just leave the entire work to other people. Let’s say you have a business development sales rep and then you outsource everything to the person but you don’t take on anything like documenting the process. That can be a con.

**3. The entrepreneur level:** Now this is a person who has built systems. What this means is that you document how to do the work, what are the outcomes that need to come out of a process, you have key performance indicators (results that signal you’re getting things right), etc. In this level you also have people that you’re leading or managing or contracting to. Also, here your thought perspective is often on the question of “if I’m building this, can I leave this work and travel for a week or two and things will continue working without my full presence?” If your answer is yes then you’ve got a good work system! A good workflow or system can be like automating your client outreach, so when you’re out there’s a software that’s still sending emails or invoice reminders in the background. Or perhaps your team members know what they’re supposed to do at every given point and you have processes and procedures for everything, like oh if a client is requesting for extra revision and you’re away, your team members would know that extra revisions cost a certain % of total fees.

Also at this stage, another question that pops to mind is *“Can I license or sell my business to other people? Can what I’ve built become a franchise?”*

You don’t need to have the intention to sell but the question will reveal to you if you built something strong enough for others to desire to be part of or want to invest in or work with you on.

For example if you have an illustration studio, can you franchise it? Can you have another person in another city, say, “I love the way you get clients and sell out these illustrations. I want to use your business name and the workflows you’ve built and then open another branch and do the same work you’re doing.”

These are the three levels of creative business ownership that you need to think about and understand what phase you are in.
