I’ve come to the end of the Grow Design Fellowship. I had a few assumptions going into it: some were correct, and some were wrong. For instance, I assumed people might drop off within the first four weeks, and they did. That’s why I selected a higher number of participants than I originally planned, but even then, the numbers still dropped below my target.

What I didn’t anticipate was the impact of the project-based structure. It was an eight-week program, and I didn’t expect an entire group to drop out and leave a client stranded. I didn’t have extra designers to put on the project, so I had to close it with that client.

Nevertheless, the other projects ran successfully. I’ve spoken with two people about it — both are working on incredible projects with the Durian Foundation and SAP — and they gave me some great advice:

\* Always define your success ratio; at the end of the day, aiming for 60 to 70 percent is realistic.

\* Always have multiple contingency plans in place.

\* Keep clients updated, even when things go wrong.

\* Just do your best.

I think I’m way better prepared for the next cohort, where I’m taking on the challenge of making it more in-person than online. One of my mistakes was not getting everyone’s phone numbers, so for the next cohort, I have to make sure I have that covered.

I was a little bit scared at first because for the second cohort, I made it more international and opened it up to a wider variety of countries. About 14 to 15 countries applied, so it was quite a challenge. For the next one, the challenge is to make the international aspect work across two specific countries.
