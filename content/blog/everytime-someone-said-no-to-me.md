*Every time someone told me no, I went ahead and built what they rejected my application for anyway*

### **2025**

Between 2024 and 2025, just before I launched the first G[rohwie Design Fellowship](https://www.grohwie.com/fellowship) (a prestigious fellowship for emerging designers), I thought I needed a lot of money to run it, so I applied for a grant. I prepared a pitch deck and every other important piece of information I thought I needed.

Did I get it? Nah, I did not. Applied twice and same story, they didn’t want to see my cool, so I counted my wins, put together my savings and submitted a call for fellowship application on Grohwie. I hadn’t even fully started a Grohwie design studio yet. I had only registered it a few days before the fellowship call went out in 2025.

When I put it out in public, trust me, I was a little scared. I had never organised an international design fellowship. But I learned! And [bootstrapped](https://medium.com/@mirabellemorah/ill-never-do-this-again-bootstrapping-an-international-design-fellowship-af2644b694e7) my first international design fellowship. 

#### **Doing the stuff I didn’t get me grants for**

In 2025 I reached out to people who could be facilitators and speakers, and even someone who then was a senior designer at Intercom reached out to me to speak. Thank you, you life saver [Inioluwa Abiodun!](https://www.linkedin.com/in/inioluwa-abiodun/)

#### **My savings were invested in others**

I put together that first fellowship with my savings, and a shout-out goes to my very dear friend Tony Joy, who also sent me some extra cash that helped onboard the 11th fellow (the original number was 10). I had 11 people become fellows, gave them stipends, and connected them with some awesome Scottish senior design directors. It was great.

Feedbacks were reeling in like:

> “I was shown a whole new world of possibilities. I realised I can do everything I set my mind to.”

> “The speakers were so real with us. They shared their actual struggles. It gave me so much hope.”

### 2026

2026, for the second fellowship I went wider and expanded my horizons. I then applied for a couple of more grants, tracked them.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1440/1*RJn77JT7xZffSuicYD6uWQ.png)

One in particular was the We Are Family Youth to the Front Fund. I was quite excited that I got to the final call stage. But after what felt like a very long time and not hearing back, I had a foreboding that I wasn’t going to get the grant.

During that application and wait season I had already started organising the second cohort of the Grohwie Design Fellowship. Because if I learned anything, it’s that it’s cool to be invited to a table but don’t wait if it’s taking too long, go create your own stuff. This time I was accepting 20 international fellows and had had close to 300 total applications since the start of the fellowship. More countries applied: over 30 countries had applied.



#### **No funding? Nobody can stop reggae!**

This was going to be longer: 8 weeks total, with 4 weeks of theory and then, for the remaining 4 weeks, fellows were going to be paired to work in other organizations to build their portfolio and do some #DesignForGood projects. Was it scary? Yes. But we did it anyway.

> When I didn’t get the grant anyway, I went ahead and did what I could, with what I had, where I was at.

#### Lessons

When I applied for most of these grants and opportunities in 2026, I applied with the plan to fund in person design residency programs for designers. I want to support designers because I believe that talent is endless but opportunities and access are not always there.

> *How do you, as a designer, think in a business way? How do you find employment? Gigs? Community? How can you also improve your confidence and work with other people? How do you also get access to work with very amazing directors who could give you advice from their wealth of knowledge? How do you find others who are cracked like you? Be limitless, be boundless, and just interact with other people.*

These are some of the questions I try to answer with emerging designers I support.

#### **I got a funding partner!**

When I didn’t get the We Are Family Foundation grant, it kind of sucked because I wanted to organize two different residency programs: one in the UK and one in Nigeria.

But a moving woman will always meet her targets with greater opportunities. As God would have it, I found support from Mastercard Foundation FAST program with Anzisha as my implementing partners. I went through a 4-month business mentorship program that took me from getting funded to build a design software to actually getting funded to organizing two in person design workshops that were incredibly fascinating: one in Calabar and one in Lagos. The feedback from people was.

The feedback was wowza! I guess this is a reminder to go for the things that you want to go for.

#### **Other accelerators I got into in 2026**

Before I forget, I was also part of different accelerator programs in the year.

- I was part of Catalyze Her by Visa Foundation.
- I was also part of the very prestigious Scottish University Converge Challenge, a very fantastic body. I got selected, was pitching for funding plus in-kind support, and didn’t make it to the finalists.
- Within that same year I also got headhunted by Founder Vine, which was by NatWest and Scale Circle. I couldn’t really complete that because a lot of things were going on in my life.
- I also applied to Entrepreneur First. I made it to the interview, 15 minutes, but they said I was too far awesome for them. That is, my business was already generating revenue and being successful, so they needed people who were in the idea stage and would be willing to just build something new with other people.

Again this is a reminder to apply for things, go build stuff even when the support doesn’t come. WHEN you don’t get what you applied for, go and get something else, but just keep building. A moving company will always hit good points.
