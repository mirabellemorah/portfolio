When I started building a studio, I thought the hard part would be the craft. It turned out the hard part was the habits around the craft: how I planned my week, how I said no, and how I looked after myself so I could keep going.

Imagine a garden. You can plant beautiful seeds, but if you never water them or pull the weeds, you only get a patch of dirt with good intentions. A creative business is much the same. The work needs care on a schedule, not only in bursts of inspiration.

Here are the habits that have helped me most, explained as simply as I can.

## Protect one quiet block

Pick one block of time each week where nothing is allowed to interrupt you. No messages, no meetings. Even two hours is enough. It is where your best thinking happens, because your mind finally has room to wander and connect ideas.

Think of it like leaving a window open in a stuffy room. Nothing dramatic happens, but everything feels clearer after a while.

## Write down what finished looks like

Before starting a project, write one sentence that describes what done means. For a short animation it might be: a 30 second video, two rounds of feedback, delivered in two formats. When the sentence is clear, the project stops growing in the background.

This is the same reason a recipe lists the number of servings. Without it, you keep cooking and nobody knows when to eat.

## Say no kindly and early

A late no is expensive. An early no is generous, because the other person can find help somewhere else. You can say no and still be warm: thank you for thinking of me, this is not the right fit right now, and I hope it goes well.

Over time, people remember the clarity more than the refusal.

## Keep a small win list

At the end of each week, write three things that went well. They can be tiny: a client replied happily, a sketch finally worked, you went for a walk when you wanted to skip it. On hard days, read the list. It reminds you that progress is real even when it is quiet.

It is like keeping a jar of marbles. Each one is small, but the jar slowly fills.

## Rest like it is part of the work

Rest is not a reward you earn after everything is finished, because everything is never finished. Build it in. A day without screens, a walk without a podcast, a proper lunch away from the desk. Your best ideas often arrive when you stop chasing them.

A phone that never charges eventually stops working, no matter how good the phone is.

## Start with one

You do not need all five at once. Choose the one that feels easiest and try it for two weeks. Habits grow best when they are small enough to repeat on a tired day.

If you try one, I would love to hear how it goes.
