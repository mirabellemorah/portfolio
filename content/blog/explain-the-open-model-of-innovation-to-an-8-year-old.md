## Thinking tech + business: what an open model of innovation means

An ***open model of innovation*** is a business strategy or an innovation strategy where a company would open up its ‘*well of knowledge’* to receive even more insights and knowledge from the public so they can improve their products or services. That means, instead of relying solely on their own research and development, they collaborate with external partners, including other companies and even customers, to find new ideas.

**There are two main types of open innovation:**

### **1. Inbound open innovation:**

This is when a company brings external ideas or technologies into their own innovation process. It’s like saying, “Hey, we don’t have all the answers, so let’s see what others are doing and use their great ideas to improve our products.” For example, a smartphone social enterprise might use a camera technology idea suggested by their customers during a hackathon, to improve its phone’s camera quality.

### **2. Outbound open innovation:**

This is when a company shares or licenses its ideas or technologies to other organizations. It’s like saying, “*We have this cool technology that we want to be open source, so let’s let others benefit from it too.*” For instance, a software company might make the codebase/repository and documentation of their software open-source, so others can contribute to it or even try to replicate it. But most importantly**, others can use it for free.**

Both these approaches allow companies to be more flexible and efficient in their innovation processes. They can tap into a wider pool of ideas and resources, potentially leading to better products and services, faster development times and new revenue streams.

> The open model of innovation is pretty much the opposite of the t**raditional closed innovation model,** where companies rely solely on their internal resources and keep their innovations and processes secret.

## Explaining the open model of innovation to an 8-year-old

In a nutshell, rather than building a toy car all by yourself (hey it gets lonely building alone!) an open model of innovation is like inviting many of your friends over to help you build your toy car. And there are two ways to make your car bigger and better — ie two types of open innovation.

***Inbound and outbound.***

Inbound open innovation is when you ask your friends to give you parts of their own toys, so you can add them to your toy car and transform it from a red, little toy car, to a bigger and better toy car (this is simply like getting ideas from outside to improve your own project).

Now, outbound open innovation is when you share pieces of your own car, or when you lend your little red car to others, so they can use it too. This time around, it may not make your car automatically bigger, but it could put you in a good friendship position where you’re also helping them have fun, and they could help you out in future too (this is like sharing your ideas with others so they can make cool things too).

In both cases, you’re working together and sharing, which can make everyone’s playtime more fun and exciting than if you all built toy cars alone.

*If you’re building a startup, think of which you want your startup to embody.*
