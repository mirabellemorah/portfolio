I’ve been [programming](https://github.com/mirabellemorah) for the past 2 years, and every single little opportunity I find, trust me, I’m always trying to find ways to merge my coding practice with my design skills.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1440/0*JP0FB0ZpdXrst8lt)

From when I first developed my first [solo website](https://github.com/mirabellemorah/girlcode) (it was looking so cool), to when I also decided to build my own [portfolio](https://www.mirabellemorah.com/) website completely using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript on my own, the story goes on. For a while, I also started reaching out to creative technologists, asking them for advice, and met some really cool, interesting people.

#### Reaching out to creative technologists

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1440/1*MdwvTRS35eYu4zsy7H2DQQ.png)

I recently attended [INTL](https://intl.international/), a design festival that brings together really super interesting creative and design minds from all over the world to Glasgow. I attended a 2-day coding workshop on building “FLEXIBLE VISUAL SYSTEMS DESIGN” with [Coding Systems](https://codingsystems.info/). This was with *“[Tim Rodenbröker](https://timrodenbroeker.de/), MA — an experienced Creative Coding teacher, and Martin Lorenz, PhD ([Martin Lorenz](https://medium.com/u/2866b0aa013b)*) *— who has been teaching and applying Flexible Visual Systems since 2001.”* And I really, really liked that. I think I just found 2 creative technology heroes.

I really wasn’t very certain what to expect. I think I wanted to either go for the poster workshop or go for the creative coding one. I’m so glad I opted for the creative coding one.

#### My experience with Coding Systems

I had sat beside a designer called [Matteo](https://www.instagram.com/matto_matteo_/). I think he lives Germany, and I was looking at his work. Already, I was quite picking up inspiration from pixel-based work, and Matteo’s work really super inspired me that I’d even asked him to teach me how he made his pixel based work.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1440/1*C8kVIEG-ws8FV8Ix2D0bpw.jpeg)

Not only that but Martin and Tim were also trying to show us how to make use of the design system that they had built, from exploring and getting references and patterns to then conceptualizing how we break those patterns down into simple components. That’s like simple design components, and then how we use pieces of code to make really amazing things (designs) from everything we’ve put together.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1440/1*kGQmowtZM1EPIPy-GF6BHw.png)

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1440/1*5qxDuIIc-E-gQ2lso6e6qg.jpeg)

I do appreciate that we had so much time on our hands during the workshop to explore and experiment. It wasn’t lots of talking, but more of answering questions, moving back and forth, then just allowing us to do a lot of work on our own and with their input and feedback and guidance. I also couldn’t even work on it at home because I had just come back really exhausted, so I continued the next day at the workshop.

[During my masters](https://blogs.ed.ac.uk/alumni-profiles/2026/11/24/mirabelle-morah/), I did a masters in business, but I focused on creative industries. I’ve been contemplating doing a PhD and I’ve had my friends constantly send me PhD applications around creative industries and technology as well. *Mhmmm…* I’ve quite enjoyed being at the workshop and INTL in general, and meeting cool people.
