> These are my personal experiences and examples.

## Reaching out online to people I don’t know

It really depends on who it is and what the relationship is like. Do they know me? How long have we known each other? I was once told to *“make friends before I need friends”* so I try to make sure it’s not awkward before reaching out to anyone for an ask.

If I do not know someone but have identified them either online or through a recommendation, and I have an immediate question, I would find their email address or LinkedIn profile, get to know them better, and use my GMD model to send them a message.

## The GMD model

- **G**: **Greetings** first, pleasantly and swiftly. A quick introduction about me could follow this.
- **M**: **My message** is next. I get to the important and main reason I’m messaging because I know people are busy, and I don’t want my core message, question or ask to be lost in a sea of text.
- **D**: **Details last and any other business.** Details would be to explain more about why I chose them and any other relevant information. I also like to make people feel no pressures at all in not obliging my requests.

The above is an example of a LinkedIn inmail I sent to someone who’d worked at/with [IDEO](https://www.ideo.com/) (a global design and innovation company) for ~20 years. There’s that saying, **“Just shoot your shot,”** and **“You miss 100% of the shots you don’t take”.** I thought this individual was really remarkable. I wanted to get their perspective, but I was worried they wouldn’t reply. But they did! And it was one of the best conversations and inisghts I’d ever had related to my research for my academic dissertation at the University of Edinburgh.

## The helpful KISS principle

Use this to streamline your message to people.

K - Keep

I - It

S - Short and

S - Simple

## Reaching out online: people you know a little and they know you too

You should engage with people online and comment on their posts if you enjoy them. Liking and reposting encourages the people you follow and keeps you on their radar. Plus, being top of the mind can lead to being at the tip of one’s tongue in case of recommendations. It’s awkward when all you do is *“ask ask ask”* things of people without being a giver yourself or just jumping out of the blue and making a request. Also, people are not obliged to give you a response, whether you know them well or not.

Below is an example of an incredible motion and 3D designer living in Berlin whom I reached out to. I genuinely adore his work**, regularly engage with and comment on his posts. When I needed some advice on technical setups for motion and 3D work, I reached out to him,** and he gave some of the best recommendations I needed.

## Meeting people in person

I grew up quite shy, and to this day, I’m one of those people who’ll probably wait for the visitor to leave the sitting room before coming out.

I’ve known and met incredible people since I was a teenager, including friends like Wadi Ben-Hirki (an award-winning philanthropist and Advocate for Gender Equity) to Grammy-award-winning singers like [Nile Rodgers](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D4E22AQFQKEW_t-ookA/feedshare-shrink_1280/feedshare-shrink_1280/0/1713147798661?e=1726099200&v=beta&t=6FqccCfuZy0gHs5LtrwfYudvPoIg4GIRd7h7eCBr_nI) and [Don Gips](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/D4E22AQEM8QVtf9mB6w/feedshare-shrink_2048_1536/0/1713139550911?e=1726099200&v=beta&t=Cg4dEAdn0gxNdIg4AmMjsXt98zkYhzLqORICTM85gaI) (CEO of the Skoll Foundation and former U.S. Ambassador to the Republic of South Africa). I keep in mind when approaching anyone that on a much deeper level, **we're all human.** I’m confident in myself, [I believe in my worth](https://www.instagram.com/p/C9uzkksI-t1/), and I know there’s something others can teach me, as I can too, irrespective of where we all are in life.

While these are from my own experience, be sure to adapt them to your unique circumstances and understand the kind of individual you are approaching. You cannot reach out to everyone in the same way.
