Today I read a little story, and specifically a phrase touched my heart the most.

Perhaps in the past, I may have, but today feels like the first day in forever that I have felt so seen, so understood by this little story that I had only begun to read.

I will share a part of the short story with you.

\*\*\*

> *“Thank you very much,”* said Parish, rather coolly.

> *“But it is not a cold, it is a fever. I should not have bothered you for a cold. And my wife is in bed downstairs already. I can’t get up and down with trays, not with my leg. But I see you are busy. Sorry to have troubled you. I had rather hoped you might have been able to spare the time to go for the doctor, seeing how I’m placed: and the builder too, if you really have no canvas you can spare.”*

> *“Of course, “* said Niggle; though other words were in his heart, which at the moment was merely soft without feeling at all kind. *“I could go. I’ll go, if you are really worried.”*

Excerpt from *“Leaf by Niggle”* a short story that was written by J. R. R. Tolkien in 1938–39.

\*\*\*

**“Of course, “ said Niggle; though other words were in his heart, which at the moment was merely soft without feeling at all kind…”**

This story made me incredibly sad, for I saw parts of myself in Niggle. The sentence in bold summed up how I often felt. Soft at heart because someone was in need and I desired to help, but internally troubled because I was unwilling to leave my station, unwilling to leave tasks I was deep within. It’s like a divide, the need to help, the call to complete important tasks that need completion, and the reminder to not let someone else’s emergencies be my emergency.

Mhmmm...

The story goes that Niggle, needed to finish his painting. He focused so much on the details of it but merely had enough time to complete it because Parish, his neighbour, and many others kept calling at Niggle, interrupting his flow and work. Needing one assistance or the other from him. Yet kind-hearted, but knowing that he had such little time left to complete his work, Niggle would go out of his way, making sacrifices to help Parish who was quite ungrateful and not as considerate as Niggle.

You see, Tolkien (the author) never pointed out exactly, that Niggle died. But by using metaphors and allegories, we find out that Niggle, eventually completed his artwork, not in this world, but in another word. For his artwork was of the most importance to him, unfortunately, he’d already passed away without completing his artwork in reality. And his work was only completed in the next world which he journeyed into.

When Niggle passed away, Councillor Tompkins labelled Niggle as someone of “**no practical or economic use**” and accused Headmaster Atkins of not perhaps, indoctrinating Niggle well enough to be a useful and **“serviceable cog of some sort**” where he would be “useful” to society even if it meant Niggle would wash the dishes, or have another communal kind of service that would make him pay taxes and make the law-makers even richer. Tompkins never valued Niggle’s art; saw it as useless and his neighbour, Parish, used the canvas to patch up his broken roof — all after Niggle died.

It made me think about the books I’d read, about MJ DeMarco’s book on “UNSCRIPTED: Life, Liberty, and the Pursuit of Entrepreneurship”.

It felt to me that Niggle, may have been living his own kind of unscripted life. One where he did not play into the ‘script of the society’. Such as go to school, be indoctrinated, get a job, consume, pay taxes, keep up with the rat race, consume more, save pennies, have children, consume more, retire, die.

He did seem law-abiding, however, paying his taxes legally I assume, but Niggle was just a little man who lived just outside of town and was kind. He enjoyed his art, and did his own things, but was often taken advantage of, and often interrupted during his work, unfortunately.

The story, in many ways, touches many different points of life. From fulfilling personal calling to selfishness, kindness, man as an economic cog, tending to the emergencies of others and neglecting one’s own needs, and much more.

Some say that this story was Tolkien’s way of passing a message across about how he could not finish some of the great stories he also had to write.

Nonetheless, I read “Leaf by Niggle” and all the meanings I picked from it made me really sad. It seems a reminder to myself, to not neglect all my own work, which needs to be tended to while I still have time on earth. For helping others is a noble task, but so is finishing up our own tasks.
