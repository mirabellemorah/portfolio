I don’t know you know? I was offered a promotion. I was already headed towards mid-senior leadership. But I just felt like I wanted to do something more in a different industry. In the creative sector. So I left.

Then I knocked on many doors, on agencies. Studios. I travelled far between cities and nations. Sat in informal interviews. Begged for design internships. Asked start-ups if I could become a creative for them. I said I had many transferable skillsets. I didn’t have the traditional design background. I wasn’t studying graphic design. But I studied literature, and later business. I studied how to tell stories. How to decipher meanings that are not said, but implied, how to make people’s hearts tingle with emotions that they didn’t know existed. I could do that with my animations and motion designs.

I studied a postgraduate degree in innovation and business. How to nudge people to make decisions. How to sell. But I always wanted to still be in the creative sector. I asked to work within the game industry, I asked to work in creative studios, again begged to take paycuts just to have experience within the creative industry.

But it just wasn’t coming through. So, I built my own table.

I went big, I organized my own international design fellowship. If I couldn’t get it? If I couldn’t get to work in these industries? With these organizations? With these agencies? Then I would help other people build up their own design and business skills. And that’s what I did. Started a fellowship. Connected young emerging designers with experts. Then I made my own table again. I started my own design studio. If you won’t let me in yours? I’ll build mine.

Had no prior experience. Just a heart that wanted to go for things. So I niched down to the areas that I knew best: Working with startups and working with purpose-led organizations. That’s how I grew. That’s how I found my own clients. That’s how my design company blossomed. I found clients from posting on LinkedIn, telling friends about my work.

And that’s just what I did.
