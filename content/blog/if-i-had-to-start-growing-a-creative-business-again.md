If I had to start growing a business again, I’d write myself a contract as soon as possible. I’d map out all the tasks I need to handle as a creative director, and once that’s done, I’d identify at least two or three other roles I would employ or contract out. That would free up my time to focus on what I can do.

I did this eventually, but when you’re starting out, you’re often broke, or at least on my end, I was bootstrapping so I had to manage the capital I had carefully, and I also wanted to reinvest profit into the business.

In the first month of running [**Grohwie**](https://www.grohwie.com), I outsourced many things. One downside was that I expected too much of people within their first few weeks, so I burned myself out. I expected the social media manager I had hired to deliver exactly the quality I wanted, and I expected the other team members to hit the ground running just a few days into the program. What I would do differently now is be more patient.

My other mistake is that I had hired people I knew from the past without proper interviews, and I let that lack of process slow me down. Learning from that, I’ve gone on to hire more contractors, but this time I know better to conduct interviews and ask the right questions.

Those are a few things I would do differently, though I think I did them reasonably well overall. My advice, if you’re starting out (or if I were starting again), would be to write out those other job descriptions and keep them at hand. It doesn’t matter if you don’t have the funds to pay anyone yet; there’s something powerful about simply being ready. As they say, prepare and your time will come. Knowing exactly what tasks need doing means you won’t end up burning yourself out by taking on more than you can handle. Those will definitely be my principles if I have to start growing again.

I have a [fellowship](https://www.grohwie.com/fellowship) project currently ongoing, and I’ve learned to divide much of the work among other people. I’m stressing less and going easier on myself, and I think that’s very important.
