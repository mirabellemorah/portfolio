## About Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program

[Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at the University of Edinburgh](https://www.linkedin.com/article/edit/7246999806634508289/#) offers both on-campus postgraduate scholarships for those who wish to study in Edinburgh (1 year) and online postgraduate scholarships for those who wish to still live in their countries but study through online distance learning. For [online scholars](https://global.ed.ac.uk/mastercard-foundation/apply-for-a-scholarship/online-postgraduate-scholarships), you get to study part-time over 3 years or you can choose to do a post-graduate diploma part-time for about 2 years. The scholarships at the moment this article is published are for Masters degrees and I have been studying as an on-campus scholar in Edinburgh, studying an [MSc in Entrepreneurship and Innovation.](https://www.ed.ac.uk/studying/postgraduate/degrees/index.php?r=site/view&edition=2024&id=897)

## What do you get as a Scholar?

Most of my explanations below will be from an on-campus scholar’s perspective, and I can provide the most context for that.

> *“All Mastercard Foundation Scholars at the University of Edinburgh will participate in a Climate Leadership Program and the scholarship as a whole is dedicated to nurturing a community of talented, entrepreneurial and compassionate leaders from across Sub-Saharan Africa.”*

As a scholar, we received full tuition fees from Mastercard Foundation, flight tickets to and fro our countries of origin, fees to register for TOEFL, visa fees paid and assistance in fixing appointments, on ground transportation assistance. On getting to Edinburgh, accommodation was kindly provided, study and living stipends, [regular events and seminars,](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mcfscholarsed_last-week-we-were-thrilled-to-welcome-reeta-activity-7138866279003631617-KlhO?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop) retreats to the [Scottish highlands](https://www.instagram.com/p/Czg4EJTNo1g/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&igsh=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==) (majestic stuff!), great pastoral care for mental health concerns and weekly check-ins or hangouts with the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program team, a laptop, a summer school in South Africa at the University of Witwatersrand, monthly business ideas competitions. I was one of two [on-campus scholar representatives](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C0jOCb2o7Pq/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&igsh=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==), so I travelled to Rwanda at the start of 2024 to attend a [Scholars Council Meeting](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mcfscholarsed_2024-mastercard-foundation-scholars-council-activity-7168639973871591426-lZYv?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop) in Kigali with other Mastercard Foundation Scholars from different continents.

## How to prepare for your application

Now that I’ve told you about the juicy part. In my calculation, the acceptance rate for on-campus scholars is anything between 0.5% to \<1%. There could be space for 35–50 people from a pool of over 6,000 applicants. But fret not! Put in that application! So long as you meet a solid amount of the requirements. [Please see the FAQs for more details](https://global.ed.ac.uk/mastercard-foundation-scholars-program/apply-scholarship/frequently-asked-questions).

## Who gets in? Do I need to have great online proof or have documented my work online before applying?

While I encourage documenting your work online, it's not exactly required. I don’t see that written as part of the requirements (please double-check yourself in case of any recent updates). I have a scholar friend who only created their LinkedIn profiles when they got their scholarship and came on campus. They rarely post on social media but have a thriving TikTok account featuring just their dance videos. But I tell you, this scholar friend is a stunning inventor!

Others don’t even appear anywhere online and prefer leading private lives. I guess their applications and interviews spoke for them and that they have other ways to document their past successes, etc., when asked for proof. I have active social media profiles, but they do not look at this much. The program recognises that many qualified people could live in rural areas without access to strong internet connections and no interest in the online world. The program is also very interested in giving scholarships to refugees who can show ambition and meet the requirements. I once met a scholar who reared cows and built schools in rural places, if I remember correctly. This individual would have other needs and interests than building an online personality. They thrive in person.

## Requirements and eligibility

Be sure to check the website [for all updated requirements](http://global.ed.ac.uk/mastercard-foundation) such as:

- Facing barriers to education and/or in your early career
- Must have done an undergraduate degree and must have a degree certificate and official final transcript to upload alongside your application
- Be residents and citizens or hold refugee status in a sub-Saharan African country
- Applicants must demonstrate a track record of leadership and service within their community
- Be under 35 years
- Must demonstrate experience, or at least ambition, in making a meaningful impact in addressing the climate crisis
- Should not have previously completed a postgraduate degree (when applying for masters programs)

## Writing your essays and knowing your why

Back to the University of Edinburgh, when you are certain that you are truly interested in a particular course, you have a history in it, have future ambitions for it, and meet the eligibility criteria, you will then be asked to answer a series of questions which could change or remain the same from year to year.

As I said earlier, I did Entrepreneurship and Innovation because I wanted to understand how to build business and organisational systems that could be replicable across other countries and tweaked to local systems. Before applying, I spent the past 6–7 years building [BlankPaperz Media](http://www.blankpaperz.com/), organising workshops, volunteering for organisations and spearheading [GC Fund](https://www.blankpaperz.com/gcfund/) to sponsor entrepreneurial and creative trainings for young people in Nigeria. BlankPaperz already had over 100,000 web users and a lot of awards and recognitions from international social enterprise bodies such as [Ashoka](https://fb.watch/uY7MIkU3Hm/), [We Are Family Foundation](https://www.wearefamilyfoundation.org/gtl-2018/mirabelle-morah) and even the [US Department of State](https://exchanges.state.gov/non-us/program/study-us-institutes-student-leaders/spotlight/susi-2017-alum-mirabelle-morah-named-2018-global-teen-leader). I was also working as the Communications Manager for the [Social Enterprise World Forum (SEWF)](https://sewfonline.com/) before my application, handling communications for all our global events. I had much working experience on the ground, but I also wanted to blend this with theory as I advanced. So I went for a business degree that balanced out practice with theory, allowed me to think socially while working on business ideas, and also a fellowship/scholars program that embedded community, giving back and leadership with academics.

Please note that I also had Scholar colleagues who came straight from undergrad to the masters program. The selection choice is always balanced.

So ask yourself, why do you want this? Understanding your “why” gives you solid ammunition to attack your essays and respond during interviews.

## Dissecting your essay questions

Say for instance, your first essay question during application is:

“Reflecting on your life so far, tell us how you meet the key selection criteria for the Scholars Program. You should include information on your academic achievements and barriers to education you have faced. We are particularly welcoming applications from those who identify as women, refugees (or displaced), and/ or those with disabilities as we understand that these groups face barriers to education. If you identify with any of these characteristics, please reflect this in your answer, with as much detail as you are comfortable sharing”.

## This is how I would approach this:

1.  Time: Begin your essays far ahead of the deadline. But if you’re Jackie Chan like some of my scholar flatmates, who say they spent just a day or two writing their applications and submitting them, then go ahead with all pleasure. Personally, I spent weeks fine-tuning my essay answers with the assistance of some friends.
2.  Word limit: You may have only 250 words to answer only 1 question. Note that this example question appears far too large to be answered in only 250 words. What I do is to unpack the question(s) and take out the subquestions inside of it.
3.  Unpacking the questions: In this example question, 3 other questions in it are:

- *How do you meet the key selection criteria (what makes you eligible? Why should we give you this scholarship? Why do you want it?)*
- *As an undergraduate what did you accomplish? (were you top of the class? Did you start a business that helped others while in school? Did you supplement your studies with informal education to keep developing? etc.)*
- *What challenges did you face in school or perhaps during the early years of your career? (Did you brave all odds, like not having school fees yet managed to graduate well? Were there national strikes? Was there a war? Mental health issues? Death of a loved one? Your personal confusion?)*

> **Note:** Now, this is not a place to dwell on sob stories of how you trekked to school for 7 years and got hit by lightening and worked in a warehouse to raise 16 children on your own, then one day you all planted trees together and made a forest to solve climate change in 22 villages. While in reality there are people who have gone through these real-life situations, I also promise you that others have not but will falsely use these stories in their essays. Flee from evil nwanne’m. Say something else unique and authentic. Let your own personality shine through. To answer this question, I went the opposite route and wrote in my application that I was fortunate to have had a good education while growing up because my father promised himself to give his children solid education. So yes, I had a good educational background. And because I understood the value of it, when I began my own initiative BlankPaperz, I also began to [educate others about the power of the media.](https://www.blankpaperz.com/about/) I partnered with other [NGOs to fund educational](https://www.instagram.com/p/CmDppFwowjQ/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&igsh=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==) and creative training programs for young people and that was my way of giving back.

## Dig deeper

Now to further attack this question, study the requirements and eligibility requirements. Also go further and research deeper about the Foundation, what is the [Foundation’s vision?](https://mastercardfdn.org/foundation/mission-vision/) What are the impacts so far? [Watch videos](https://fb.watch/uY8s_PoVc8/) about the [CEO](https://mastercardfdn.org/people/reeta-roy/) or other alumni speaking. What’s something they say stood out for them in the prorgam?

After building a deep understanding of not just what the question requires of you but also what the Foundation and even the [University of Edinburgh](https://www.ed.ac.uk/education/about-us/mission#:~:text=deep%2Drooted%20and%20distinctive%20internationalism,priority%20for%20individuals%20and%20communities) look out for in students and applicants. Now write your answers to the unpacked questions and keep the [STAR approach](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method) (situation, task, action, result) in mind. This is not the point where you fully engage the STAR approach yet. Just write out all answers as it is in your head. Pour it all out on the paper (or word document) so you have all the points, and write about how you feel the Foundation’s and University’s missions and values align with yours. I know this sounds like a lot, but don’t worry, write it out and don’t even try to edit your work now. Let your passion flow. Repeat for other questions.

## What STAR stands for

**situation** — the situation you had to deal with  
**task** — the task you were given to do  
**action** — the action you took  
**result** — what happened as a result of your action and what you learned from the experience

[Source: UK National Careers Services](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method)

## CARL framework

You can also use the CARL framework to answer questions about reflecting.

C — context  
A- Action  
R- Result  
L — Learning

## Cutting down your answers (with my real life essay example)

Now you may have written over 1,000 words to answer this 250-word-essay. Don’t fret. After writing all your answers as you recall them. Rest your eyes and brain for a couple of hours or days then return with fresh eyes. This is where the STAR approach comes into play, you have identified these 3 other questions within 1 question. How do you make all your points relate to each other and tell a coherent story? The below is an excerpt from my own essay.

## Unpacking my own essay example for you to understand it better

Firstly I have a B.A (Hons) in English and Literary Studies from the University of Calabar, and I am switching to the business school for a masters in Entrepreneurship and Innovation.

**Stay with me.**

Within entrepreneurship and innovation, I am very interested in creative media, in storytelling, from my natural abilities. Chimamanda Adichie also deeply inspired me as a young writer and after reading her novel [Purple Hibiscus](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Purple-Hibiscus-Chimamanda-Ngozi-Adichie/dp/0007189885) in secondary school, I was so fascinated by how powerful stories are in amplifying important social issues in Nigeria. So I started BlankPaperz Media at 17 to also tell [stories of innovative](https://assembly.malala.org/stories/the-stories-of-young-african-writers) things my friends and other Africans were working on ([watch my 1 minute micro documentary on this here](https://youtu.be/VL-UpFo89eM?si=7EIpt1OXLOn-Jub8)). See? I am already in the line of creative business!

Now I didn’t just say I wanted to go into any kind of business, I was interested in businesses that put [people and planet first](https://peopleandplanetfirst.org/) — hence, social entrepreneurship. Also note I was working for the Social Enterprise World Forum as a communications manager then, handling social media, some PR, creating designs, etc. So I was already influenced by this method of doing business.

## How did I analyze the history of this interest in my application?

I went back to my undergrad and talked about how I was part of a school association called [ENACTUS](https://enactus.org/) as a media person. ENACTUS teaches students to find innovative business solutions to societal issues, making life easier. I used terms like circular economy and doughnut economics to show I was familiar with the entrepreneurship and innovation industry, and I enjoyed working in the media and communications arm of organisations that enjoyed doing good.

Also, remember that the Scholars program focuses a lot on climate leadership, so I emphasized the parts of my work that contributed to this.

## Piecing together your own essay

Now that you have seen how I unpacked my own answers, it’s your turn.

Try as much as possible to cut down your answers to only leave the relevant bits. You may still be stuck at 600 words, especially if you have done a lot. Don’t worry. Reduce it bit by bit. Also be very brutal. The other essay questions also give you space to mention other aspects of your experience, so only mention what is important. And because the other essay questions allow you to mention other things, try not to repeat the same answers or points in other parts. And if you do, find ways to tie them together.

Oh you are interested in storytelling? Great, maybe in the next essay question about your future plans, you can write about how you want to use your combined experience in storytelling and working with NGOs to work on a design service that caters specifically to helping organisations amplify their impact and find design talents (yep that’s right! This was actually what my postgrad capstone [dissertation, Grohwie](http://www.grohwie.com) was about).

## DON’T BE EVERYWHERE

I encourage people to stick to one thing or have a thematic focus. Do not apply as a musician who also sews clothes and wants to open an auto mechanic shop after getting the scholarship. That’s very disjointed. Have an aim, a focus, and ambitions that tally.

And speaking of ambitions, if you feel you have not accomplished much, that’s alright too. Remember that one of the requirements is to demonstrate experience, or at least ambition, in making a meaningful impact in addressing the climate crisis. So with your past experiences (internships and volunteer work count), write about your ambitions and how you can use your past knowledge and new knowledge to solve 1 or 2 specific problems. Don’t say you want to solve everything. Are you King Kong or Wonder Woman? Even the Dalai Lama had specific issues he was most interested in.

## Other tips

1.  Show, don’t just tell
2.  Include numbers about your impact where necessary (if applicable)
3.  Add one or two short quotes from others that buttresses or hits home a point you’re trying to drive about yourself
4.  Have someone else review your essay for better insights
5.  Make your essay about yourself too, let your light shine forth. We know you’re selfless and have won 200 awards, but those awards do not matter as much as who you are as an individual and what the program would mean to you. What made you win those awards or get those recognitions? That’s what your reviewer probably wants to know. The actions that got you the results. This isn’t an application or contest about who has won the most awards. Mention the most relevant recognitions, big and small but don’t dwell solely on them.
6.  Watch out for Mastercard Foundation Scholars who would also be holding free online sessions to help new applicants scale through essay questions, etc. Those who do that are sent from heaven.
7.  Don’t expect people to answer you when you message them for guidance. Respect and understand that a ton of people are very busy and while they would love to assist you personally, a million and one things are happening in their lives and so they can only signpost you to resources that have helped them.
